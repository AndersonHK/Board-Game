# Quest 004 Findings

Evidence files: [simulation log](./07-simulation-log.txt), [rulings log](./08-rulings-log.txt), [source map](./00-source-map.md)

## What Held Up

- The quick start now does real work. Compared with earlier passes, far less of the session had to be carried by hidden design memory.
- The quick-start escalation rules are much healthier than the older prototype gap. Clear-scene progress plus unresolved-hurdle pressure was enough to reach escalation 10 without inventing a separate cadence rule.
- The player-facing class text mostly held. The Warrior reward ladder was easy to compute in play, the Wizard hand-cap asymmetry remained legible, and the Cleric rescue trigger produced strong table drama again.
- Blocking-hurdle language from the quick start was good enough to run enchantments, chains, and gates without needing a new subsystem.
- Tightening the agenda thresholds helped. This time two players succeeded and two failed, which is much more informative than the all-success pattern from Quest 003.

## What Broke Or Stayed Ambiguous

- Four-player class selection was a real quick-start gap at the start of the run. This session justified promoting an explicit duplicate-class instruction for 4-player tables.
- Action order is still invisible to human players. The quick start teaches action counts but not turn sequencing.
- Prestige scoring is still only half there. The quick start promises hidden agenda pressure but still does not explain a full endgame credit procedure.
- Reaching escalation 10 during a non-final scene is still ambiguous. The session needed a ruling to avoid interrupting `Brood Wyrm Hunt` mid-resolution.
- The prototype action pool is still not a real deck list, so the game remains rules-testable before it is printtest-ready.

## What Should Be Defined Next

- Clarify action order inside an action turn in a player-facing place.
- Define how prestige or scene credit is actually counted at the end of the game.
- Clarify what happens if escalation reaches 10 during a scene that is not already the final ordeal.
- Clarify whether the Wizard's forced-discard cancel can protect any player or only the Wizard.

## What Should Be Adjusted

- Keep using duplicate-Warrior four-player sims. That tension still exposes selfish play cleanly.
- Keep the tighter agenda thresholds or something close to them. The failure rate was much more useful.
- Keep designing cards so their own text carries allied-entity rules; the quick start should not become a second card glossary if the cards can teach themselves.

## Promotion Call

- Promoted from this run: duplicate-class permission in the quick start.
- Worth documenting soon, but not yet canonizing: action-order procedure and escalation-10 mid-scene handling.
- Not ready to promote yet: the temporary Spotlight scoring proxy and the prototype action-pool-as-catalog assumption.
